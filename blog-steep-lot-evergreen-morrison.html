Mountain Builds

Building a Deck on a Steep Lot in Evergreen & Morrison, CO

If you own property in Evergreen, Morrison, Conifer, Genesee, or anywhere in the foothills west of Denver, you already know the backyard is rarely flat. Steep grades, exposed granite, mature pines, and limited access are the norm rather than the exception — and they are the reason a lot of homeowners get quoted a smaller deck than they wanted, or get turned down altogether. Mountain deck construction is a different discipline from building on a flat lot in the metro, and this is what actually changes.

Why Standard Framing Doesn’t Work on a Slope

A standard deck assumes a reasonably level yard and uniform soil: dig footings to frost depth, pour concrete, set posts, frame. On a steep Colorado lot almost none of that holds. Grade can drop several feet across the footprint, soil depth varies wildly from one hole to the next, and the load path has to be engineered rather than assumed.

Building for the slope means tall support posts, footings sized for the actual grade and the actual soil or rock conditions at each location, and framing designed to carry the deck’s full load off the terrain itself — not around it. Beam spans, post sizing, and lateral bracing all change once a deck stands well above grade, and it is common on these builds for no two footings to be identical.

This is also where the shortcut shows up. It is far easier to talk a homeowner into a smaller, simpler footprint tucked against the house than to engineer a deck that reaches out over the drop — which is usually the whole reason you want a deck on a mountain lot in the first place. A steep lot should not cost you the view.

When the Ground Is Solid Rock

Plenty of lots around Morrison, Evergreen, Golden, Genesee, and Lookout Mountain have very little soil over granite. Standard concrete footings assume you can excavate to depth; on rock there is nothing to excavate, and a poured footing sitting on the surface does not do the job.

On a recent project near Morrison we built a large second-floor-attached deck straight into a granite hillside — very little soil, mostly solid rock.

Rather than standard concrete footings, we anchored custom footers directly into the granite using specialized structural attachments. Done properly, rock is an excellent thing to build on: it does not settle, shift, or heave the way soil does through a Colorado freeze-thaw cycle. It just takes the right hardware, and a crew willing to do the harder version of the job.

Snow Load Is Not the Same as It Is in Denver

Elevation changes the math. Evergreen, Conifer, Kittredge, and the surrounding foothills sit well above the metro area, and they see heavier, wetter snow than Denver proper. Framing has to be sized for those loads — joist spacing, beam sizing, and post capacity all get affected. A deck framed to a flatland spec and carried up the hill is under-built for where it ends up, and roofed structures such as pavilions and covered decks are where this matters most.

Permits on a Foothills Lot

Evergreen is unincorporated, so deck permits are issued through Jefferson County Planning & Zoning rather than a local town building department. Nearly all new decks, expansions, replacements, and even repairs require a permit.

Much of the area also sits inside the county’s Wildland Urban Interface Overlay District, which can affect material choices and clearance requirements around the structure.

Steep or rocky lots can trigger a geotechnical review, and properties on well and septic carry setback requirements that affect where a deck can legally go. We handle that review as part of the design process and manage permitting start to finish — and because requirements change, we confirm current rules directly with the county before a design is finalized.

One thing worth checking early: a Morrison mailing address does not automatically mean the Town of Morrison issues your permit. A good deal of the surrounding area is unincorporated Jefferson County, and which jurisdiction reviews your plans changes the process. We confirm this before design work begins. Full detail on foothills permitting lives on our Evergreen deck builder page and our Conifer deck & pergola page.

Why We Take These Projects On

We regularly take on jobs that other contractors would rather pass up — steep grades, rock instead of soil, tricky access, and layouts that take real engineering to get right. Difficult terrain is a specialty here, not a limitation we work around.

Part of that is how we work: Gary handles construction and full 3D design, so you see the deck on your actual slope before anything is built, and the framing plan gets solved on screen instead of in the field. Steep-lot builds have more variables than flat ones, and seeing the finished structure in three dimensions ahead of time is how those variables stop being surprises.
