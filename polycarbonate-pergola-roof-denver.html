Already have a pergola that leaves you soaked in a rainstorm and baking in August? You don't need a new structure. We install the Hercules polycarbonate roof system onto existing pergolas across Denver and the foothills — most in a single day.

An uncovered pergola is a fair-weather structure. Once it's roofed, a rainy afternoon stops ending the party — and outdoor furniture, cushions, and a grill can live out there without being hauled in and out.

Denver sits in one of the most hail-prone corridors in the country. Multiwall polycarbonate is built to take an impact that would wreck an acrylic or vinyl panel, which is why we won't install anything lighter here.

The panels block UV while still passing daylight through, so the patio stays bright and the room behind it doesn't go dim. Tinted bronze knocks down heat and glare the way sunglasses do.

The Product

Why We Install Only Hercules for Colorado Weather

A roof on the Front Range takes more abuse in a year than most parts of the country hand out in five. Ninety-degree summer afternoons and UV at altitude, hailstorms that arrive with almost no warning between May and September, and heavy wet snow that piles up and sits there for days. A panel has to handle the whole range, not just one part of it.

That is why we install the Hercules system and nothing else. It is a multiwall polycarbonate built around 8mm four-wall standing-seam panels. The four-wall structure is what gives the sheet its rigidity under snow load, and the standing seam is what makes the finished roof leakproof rather than merely water-resistant. The cheaper corrugated and single-wall panels sold for retrofits crack under hail, yellow and go brittle from UV exposure, and split at the fastener holes after a few Colorado seasons. Replacing a failed roof costs more than installing the right one once.

Panels come in tinted bronze, translucent clear, and reflective white. Bronze is what most of our customers choose — it cuts heat and glare on a July afternoon while still letting you see sky through the roof. White reflects the most heat of the three but is opaque. Clear passes the most light and reduces temperature the least, which suits a shaded lot or a north-facing patio where heat is not the problem.

Does Your Pergola Need to Be Modified?

Your Pergola Needs Slope — and If It Doesn't Have Any, We Add It

A polycarbonate roof has to shed water. The Hercules system requires a minimum 2% slope to stay under its leakproof warranty — that's 1 inch of drop for every 48 inches of length. Without it, water pools on the panels, algae takes hold in the standing water, and the warranty doesn't apply.

Plenty of pergolas around Denver were built dead flat, because a slat-top pergola never needed to drain. That's not a dealbreaker. There are two ways we create slope on an existing structure, and which one we use depends on how your pergola is built and what you want it to look like when we're done.

METHOD 1

Build Up From the Top

Tapered shims or a new run of top slats go on over the existing wood, stepping down in height to create the fall. Nothing structural moves, and it's the faster of the two.

METHOD 2

Lower the Front Posts

We temporarily support the structure, cut the front posts down, and reset the front at the new height. More labor, but nothing is added overhead. At 2% the drop is slight — a 12-foot-deep pergola falls about 3 inches across the whole span, which takes a level to see.

Either approach gets you to the 2% the warranty requires, and we'll tell you which one fits your structure at the site visit. We check slope in several places while we're there, since a patio that slopes away from the house can make a flat pergola look like it drains when it doesn't.

Want to see how the shims work? Our polycarbonate roof cost guide has diagrams of both shim layouts, instructions for measuring your own slope with a four-foot level, and two real projects priced line by line.
